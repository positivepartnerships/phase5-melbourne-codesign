§ 01 · Existing PP groups & teams

The groups already inside the tent.

Internal reference groups and teams Positive Partnerships already convenes — each with its own purpose, cadence, and history of co-design contribution.

6 inputs
Internal reference group PP AAG

Autistic Advisory Group.

Six members offering advice across content development, workshop processes, and delivery. Members include Victoria, Geraldine, Jared McLaughlin, Kate Betts, Annalie Palachay and Nathan Watts — with diversity across education, NDIS, early childhood and lived experience.

Role in co-design
  • Reviews content development and decisions on workshop processes
  • Brainstorms opportunities and emerging directions
  • Talking-heads video content has been recorded with the group
  • Grounds the work (e.g. the PDA statement, the red/green participation language)
Engagement considerations
  • Currently meets once a term, plus extraordinary meetings as required
  • Vanessa flagged value in rethinking the role of the AAG for Phase 5
Internal reference group FNRG

First Nations Reference Group.

A group whose role has evolved from think-tank to hands-on co-design and on-the-ground delivery. All members have lived experience (personal or familial). Supported internally by the PP First Nations framework group.

Role in co-design
  • Content review and workshop scheduling review
  • Community engagement — e.g. Annie Mel coordinating Tasmania workshops; a member joining remote Broome communities in June
  • Project development (e.g. the co-designed Sensory Animation resource, the Voices book)
  • Regular online check-ins, plus working meetings on specific goals and opportunities
Engagement considerations
  • Group is keen to collaborate more broadly with PP — and reported strong synergy meeting with the AAG and the Multicultural Collaboration Group
  • “Nothing about us without us” named as a shared frame
Consider external groups SNAICC · ACCO
Internal reference group MCG

Multicultural Collaboration Group.

A 20+ strong group of professionals from different cultures and multilingual backgrounds. Supported internally by the PP multicultural engagement group. “We don’t plan any resource or workshop without their involvement.”

Role in co-design
  • Review content for cultural appropriateness — that it’s neuro-affirming, respectful, and reflects current conversations in culture
  • Work alongside NAATI-certified staff on translated resources
  • Shape how content is delivered (rather than what is delivered)
  • Example: sexuality content with Mandarin/Cantonese-speaking communities — conducted online for cultural appropriateness, with culturally relevant analogies (no “birds and bees”)
Consider external groups Other multicultural and community-led organisations to be scoped
Internal delivery network DTM

Delivery Team Members.

PP’s national delivery team — the people in the rooms with educators and families across Australia.

Role in co-design
  • Range of delivery team across Australia, with diverse professional and personal experience
  • Drawn from organisations and long-standing relationships
  • Know what is working and what is not in current delivery — valuable insight into the content process
Internal

Broader PP Team.

The wider PP staff team beyond the named reference groups — flagged as essential input not to be left out of the picture.

Standing consultative groups

State & Territory Planning Groups.

Made up of representatives from different education sectors, disability organisations, autistic-led organisations, family organisations, and NGO/service providers.

Role in co-design
  • Representatives from Catholic, independent and government education sectors

End users

Participants — the four cohorts.

The audiences PP is delivering for in Phase 5.

Cohorts
  • Educators
  • Families
  • School Leaders
  • Students
Voice-capture mechanism

Focus groups — 3-4 months post-workshop.

An opportunity to capture participant voice once they’ve had time to apply (or not) what they took from the workshop, surfaced as a possible input for the co-design process.

Voice-capture mechanism

§ 04 · Intersectionality & complex needs

Voices that have to be visible — not hidden in “and others”.

A specific call from the room: authentic engagement with cohorts who represent advanced complexity and intersectional experience needs to be visible in the process, not buried in “this group also includes”.

5 input groups
Why this matters

The conversation flagged the need for authentic engagement with those who represent advanced representation and complex needs — visible across the process, and not assumed to be implicit in other reference groups.

For teachers and specialists working in specialist settings, this visibility is also what builds buy-in: “we’re not going to get that unless we can authentically say we’ve done that work.”

Complex needs voice

Intellectual disability.

Parents, carers and individuals where autism intersects with intellectual disability — flagged as often left out of the “ADHD + autism” co-occurrence conversation.

Complex needs voice

Complex communication needs.

Voices from families and individuals across the range of complex communication needs — ensuring representation across the whole spectrum, not just verbal / high-masking experiences.

Health intersection

Autistic Doctors International.

Reference point for the co-occurring health conditions that affect autistic students — POTS, MCAS, Ehlers-Danlos and the fatigue load that intensifies in high school with hormonal change. Contact available within the team.

Mental health intersection

League of Autistic Psychologists.

LoPAC — consultation point on the autism / mental health intersection, including the more prominent mental health concerns seen in later-diagnosed autistic adults.

Identity intersection

LGBTQIA+ intersection.

People identifying as autistic are more likely than the general population to also identify on the LGBTQIA+ spectrum — an intersection to hold deliberately in the design and the engagement.

§ 05 · Research & academic

2 input streams
Via AutismCRC

Researchers.

Links to universities and researchers via AutismCRC. AERO’s education guidance team also brings researchers from across Australia into play. The challenge: maintain a research-translation focus that pulls out rich insight, experience and wisdom usable on the ground in schools and teacher training.

§ 06 · Government as approver

The final sign-off — designing for the bureaucratic frame.

A cautionary frame the team kept returning to: the government will be signing off on the final product, and we need to give them what they need to turn it around quickly.

Bureaucratic frame

The government will look at Phase 5 through the lens of the evaluation deliverables — are teachers, parents and leaders moving from one knowledge/capability point to another? They are not subject-matter experts, which makes visible alignment to frameworks and clear program logic essential.

The shift away from the current Diversity Wheel / Planning Tool product is significant; the team will need to be able to show, with referencing or embedded alignment, how the new approach traces back to the National Autism Strategy, DSE, and other relevant frameworks.

The team’s confidence: with the themes captured in the Ideas page, this can be demonstrated on paper without regurgitating the frameworks — by saying the workshop is aligned with the principles in the NAS (strengths-based, neurodiversity-affirming) and mentioning the relevant frameworks at the right moments.

Practical asks the team named: capture the process, develop a program-logic chain (if we build this knowledge or skill, this is likely to lead to that behaviour change), and have supporting resources and links ready rather than relying on regurgitation.

§ 07 · Engagement principles

How the room said we should hold this.

Cross-cutting principles for how to engage these inputs across the process.

01

Make sure representation is visible and explicit, not buried in “this group also includes”.

02

Bring autistic-led and other organisations on the journey so they end up alongside and championing — not surprised and agitating.

03

Nothing about us without us — named as a shared frame across First Nations and autistic communities in the conversation.

04

Different stakeholders need different timing — co-design early; communication updates later; specialised cohort engagement once the Service 1/2/5 content informs First Nations and DC content.

05

Be strategic about where to streamline — many peak bodies are already in State & Territory Planning Groups; engage via Autism Alliance / NABA rather than duplicating.

06

Maintain a research-translation focus — pull rich insight out of academic research into something teachers can use in the classroom.

07

Use the AAG, FNRG and MCG not just for consultation but for active delivery roles — building on the SA Autism Lead Teachers model.

08

Give government a clear program-logic chain so they can turn drafts around quickly — aligned, not regurgitated.
