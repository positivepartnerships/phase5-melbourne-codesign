§ 01 · Content depth

Content depth & representation

  • More on female autism, masking, internalised presentations
  • Broader representation of non-verbal autistic people requiring high-level support
  • More on gender non-conforming students and autism
  • More content for non-teaching participants & specialist staff
  • Almost all photos were of white people, except on the diversity slide
  • More integration and support around Aboriginal students with autism
§ 02 · Practical classroom strategies

Practical, classroom-ready strategies

most-frequent theme
  • A bank of evidence-based strategies with a short description, contexts, and age groups
  • Concrete strategies for replacing behaviours, de-escalation, regulation
  • More sensory strategies and how to better accommodate them in the classroom
  • More on EF with practical strategies — this is where most adjustments happen
  • Model strategies with video clips of their use to support staff with fewer skills
§ 03 · Age range & context

More for secondary, early years & complex cases

  • Bias toward primary settings — need more high-school examples
  • A workshop for children 5 and under
  • Early childhood foundations linked to primary-aged students
  • Case studies involving autistic students with intellectual disability
  • Strategies for high-behaviour-needs students in mainstream classrooms
§ 04 · Intersections

Intersections with ADHD, PDA & co-occurring

  • Discuss the link/relationship between autism and ADHD
  • Information on PDA (pathological demand avoidance)
  • Discussion of ODD/PDA as aspects of autism
  • How to support multiple autistic students with complex needs in one class
  • More on functions of behaviour, interoception, regulation
§ 05 · Pacing & time

Less rush — more time, more breaks

quantified above
  • Content was ‘rushed’ through — either spread to 3 days or trim to 2
  • Day 2 felt content-heavy; shorter sessions after lunch
  • 1.5 hours should be max per session
  • Brain/movement breaks consistently requested across delivery formats
  • More time for table discussion, peer sharing, problem-solving
§ 06 · Interactive & engaging

More interactive, less lecture

  • More think-pair-share, more table-discussion time
  • More hands-on activities, role-play, more frequent speaker changeover
  • Mentimeter or similar to reduce talk-time and gather responses quickly
  • Less video on Day 2, more practical strategies and brain breaks
  • School-based interactions, less ‘what is Autism’
§ 07 · Presentation & visuals

Slides, books, sound & readability

  • Slides too small; questions on screen rather than verbalised
  • Coloured workbooks — book printed in colour
  • White writing on green/grey hard to read
  • Booklet aligned with presentation, with page numbers and a TOC
  • Sound amplification for large rooms; subtitles for back of room
§ 08 · Pre & post-workshop

Continuity — before, during & after

  • Online module navigation is clunky — play/pause/skip needed
  • Online module covered a lot of Day 1 — build on rather than repeat
  • Follow-up Zoom 4 weeks after to check implementation
  • Email with all relevant links sent to attendees after
  • An advanced/Day 3 option for experienced participants
§ 09 · Light touches

Small but appreciated

  • Coloured workbooks
  • Thumb drive with resources
  • More chocolates on the table
  • Subtitles at the top of the screen for people at the back
  • Updated statistics — recent data rather than 2019

Suggestions for improvement — themed clusters ~110 suggestion items in the file
§ 01 · Time & pacing

Longer workshops, less rush

most-mentioned
  • 1-day was not enough — 2 days strongly preferred
  • Content felt rushed; would have liked more time to talk about strategies
  • Pace was too fast — missed parts while reading the slides
  • Videos ran very fast — slowing the voiceover would help
  • Extend duration so there’s time for in-depth conversation
§ 02 · Practical strategies

Strategies for behaviour, regulation & daily life

  • How to manage meltdowns, swearing, eating issues
  • More behaviour examples and strategies, more emotional regulation tips
  • Strategies for stimming, masking and high-school-age behaviour
  • More de-escalation strategies for when the child has ‘flipped their lid’
  • Ideas/resources to prevent the behaviour before it occurs
§ 03 · Adolescent & secondary content

Less primary, more high school

  • Workshop felt directed at primary/early years — high school families need their own focus
  • Adolescent-specific examples and content
  • Strategies for kids at the start of high school
  • A separate workshop for parents of high-school-age students
  • What school will look like if the child is mainstreamed
§ 04 · Local supports

Connect us to services & next steps

  • List of local services and professional supports
  • Steps for applying to the NDIS / what to do next
  • More local workshops in our area
  • Knowledge of local and national supports for day-to-day life
  • Networking opportunities in local area
§ 05 · Workbook & resources

Better workbook, clearer materials

  • A bigger workbook with all the slides so we can follow along
  • Workbook better coordinated with content covered
  • Slides should reflect the page numbers in the workbook
  • Glossary of key terms (e.g. high masking, double empathy)
  • Some slides were missing from the book
§ 06 · Parent community

Connect us with each other

  • Opportunities to connect afterwards and share experiences
  • More time for open communication and sharing between parents
  • 6-month follow-up to address issues we’ve had implementing actions
  • Standard follow-up email with relevant links in one place
  • Make space for everyone to write down one strategy that’s helped
§ 07 · Family / school partnership

When the relationship is strained

  • More on parent / teacher / school partnership when it has broken down
  • Strategies to work with the school to support my son
  • More time spent on improving home and school relationships
  • Hear from teachers about positive partnerships with parents
  • More guidance on advocating successfully at school
§ 08 · The Planning Tool

More examples, more guidance

  • More examples of the filled-out planning tool
  • A column ensuring the problem is discussed with the child
  • List of prompting questions to help fill in the planning tool
  • Needed an extra hour to complete the planning tool with guidance
§ 09 · Pre-reading & advanced

Set expectations, offer depth

  • Advertise more clearly so people know it’s intro vs advanced
  • Describe the workshop content in the advertisement
  • An advanced workshop for those who already understand the foundations
  • Pre-reading on sensory profiles before the session
  • Email after the workshop listing all relevant links
