§ 06 Direction

Content direction by service.

For each Service: the substantive content moves we'd recommend, with where to look for further detail in the source documents.

For Service 1 · Professional Learning · educators 12,000educators / yr
  1. 01Open with the ECEC pivot from day one.

    The DSE Amendment commences 1 August 2026 — extending the Standards to early childhood services (preschools, kindergartens, day care, OSHC) for the first time. A dedicated ECEC educator stream should be live in Phase 5's first six months.

    Where to look→DSE 25 Sch 1 items 1–20
  2. 02Name consultation literacy as a single transferable PL skill.

    The duty to consult repeats across DSE (s 3.5 + every Part), NCCD (evidence area 1), APST (7.3) and the NAS (co-design). Teach it explicitly with practice and assessment, not as background.

    Where to look→DSE 2005 s 3.5
  3. 03Adopt the AutismCRC F-Words framework as the adjustments vocabulary.

    The S&S 2024 Framework uses the F-Words from the ICF: Fitness, Functioning, Friends, Family, Fun, Future. Mapping DSE adjustments and NCCD levels through F-Words replaces the deficit lexicon with one child-functional vocabulary.

    Where to look→S&S 2024 Foundations p.5
  4. 04Reproduce the Statement of Rights verbatim in PL materials.

    The DSE 25 Amendment inserts a "Rights given by this Part" box at the head of Parts 4–8. Reproducing them visibly reframes the conversation from "provider obligation" to "student right".

    Where to look→DSE 25 ss 4.1A–8.1A
  5. 05Align every module to the autism microcredential from day one.

    The Australian Government's autism microcredential runs parallel to PP and Service 6B requires complementarity. Share metadata, learning records and assessment evidence from day one — not as a retrofit.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 4 + B.1.97
  6. 06Build paraprofessional content as a discrete stream.

    The RFT names "strategies for working effectively with paraprofessionals" as required content. Aides carry a large share of in-class implementation but are usually treated as recipients of teacher-led PD — give them their own stream.

    Where to look→RFT B.1.99 content focus
For Service 2 · Parent & Carer Workshops 3,000parents/carers / yr
  1. 01Add an NCCD-for-Families module.

    Every Autistic child's adjustments are being recorded against NCCD's four levels and four categories each year — family voice is one of four evidence elements. Most families have never heard of NCCD. A short, plain-language module turns subjects into partners.

    Where to look→NCCD Guidelines 2025 Evidence Area 1
  2. 02Teach the SAC goal-setting tests as a parent skill.

    The SAC Guideline anchors every supports plan in four tests — is the goal relevant, safe, desired, feasible? Give parents the four tests as a scaffold for school adjustment conversations.

    Where to look→SAC 2022 Section 2 (Goal Setting)
  3. 03Distinguish assent from informed consent — teach both.

    SAC Rec 11: every child has the right to say no (assent) — words or otherwise. SAC Rec 12: parents have the right to information they need to make informed choices. Different rights, both essential.

    Where to look→SAC 2022 Recs 11–12 · p.7
  4. 04Name parents' own rights under DDA s 4, not just rights for their child.

    The DDA protects "associates" — spouse, relative, carer — from discrimination based on a person's disability. Parents hold their own rights, not just rights via the child. Most family workshops miss this.

    Where to look→DDA 1992 s 4 (associate) + Part 2 Div 4
  5. 05Give families the Statement of Rights as a working tool.

    The DSE 25 Statement of Rights boxes are written for the person whose rights they describe. Send families home with the boxes verbatim — printed and annotated — plus a short "how to use this in a meeting" guide.

    Where to look→DSE 25 ss 4.1A–8.1A
  6. 06Centre family wellbeing as a focal area in its own right.

    SAC Section 3 names family wellbeing as one of nine focal support areas — distinct from supporting the child or training advocacy. Treat it as a named workshop strand: the load of being an associate, mental health, siblings, finances.

    Where to look→SAC 2022 Section 3 · p.72
For Service 3 · Whole School Community 15 → 20schools · 12-month engagement
  1. 01Build a discrete leadership stratum within Service 3.

    RFT 3A requires PL for the leadership team. PP has historically been thinnest there and classroom inclusion decays without it. A coherent leader stream aligned to APSP's five Practices and NCCD's Approved Authority duties closes the gap.

    Where to look→APSP 3 Requirements × 5 Practices
  2. 02Make anti-bullying a stand-alone whole-school workshop.

    DSE Part 8 has no unjustifiable hardship exception. NAS: 68.9% of Autistic 5–20 year-olds report difficulty at school. A stand-alone workshop covering peer culture, masking, online safety and Part 9 associates is overdue.

    Where to look→DSE 2005 Part 8 + Part 9 · NAS pp.14–17
  3. 03Treat the 12-month action plan as a co-designed artefact.

    The RFT requires schools to develop an action plan. Co-design it with school leaders, staff, parents/carers and (where appropriate) students — don't hand it down from PP. Provenance matters.

    Where to look→RFT 3A · PWDA Co-Design Programming Overview
  4. 04Map the school champion role to NCCD coordinator duties.

    The RFT names a school "champion" carrying continuity between PP visits. Most schools' inclusion or learning support coordinator already carries NCCD coordination. Mapping the two gives a sustained point of expertise and a known counterpart.

    Where to look→NCCD Guidelines 2025 · Approved Authority duties
  5. 05Use S&S's "deconstructing exclusion" as the pre-assessment lens.

    S&S 2024 Section 8 names "deconstructing exclusion" as an implementation imperative — most frameworks don't address why exclusion happens. Use this lens in S 3 pre-assessments instead of "what training do you need".

    Where to look→S&S 2024 Section 8 · p.52
  6. 06Plan the action plan against the SAC goal-tests.

    The four SAC goal-tests (relevant, safe, desired, feasible) that frame parent advocacy in S 2 should also frame school action plans in S 3 — same vocabulary, both sides of the partnership.

    Where to look→SAC 2022 Section 2 · p.62
For Service 4 · First Nations Outreach 10 + 2sessions + ACCO partnerships / yr
  1. 01Position ACCOs as co-deliverers, not partners.

    Co-deliverer status — actively delivering workshops, not just informing them — is the practical test of capability transfer. Build the operational mechanics (revenue sharing, accreditation, co-presenter models) from year 1, not "earned" over time.

    Where to look→RFT 4C · Closing the Gap Priority Reform 3
  2. 02Adopt S&S's First Nations approach as the assessment foundation.

    S&S 2024 has a substantial "Partnering with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, families and communities" section grounded in lived experience and the IAHA Cultural Responsiveness Framework. Adopt it as the foundation — not as adaptation of a generic PP framework.

    Where to look→S&S 2024 pp.7–11
  3. 03Standing forum seats for FPDN and NACCHO.

    FPDN (First Peoples Disability Network) and NACCHO (peak for ACCHOs) should have standing seats in Service 7 forums, with their feedback appearing in the Annual Report.

    Where to look→RFT 7B · FPDN + NACCHO
  4. 04Treat translation as upstream content design.

    Write concepts that translate well, rather than translating after the fact. Consistent with IAHA's "two-worlds" principle, and produces sharper English too.

    Where to look→IAHA Cultural Responsiveness Framework
  5. 05Design for travel, not metro defaults.

    ≥60% regional/remote target is a workforce, logistics and content design constraint. Assume one-day-on-Country windows; require community-network relationships in facilitators; flex around sorry business, NAIDOC, remote school terms.

    Where to look→RFT Att. A · S 4B target · §01 above
  6. 06Measure cultural safety separately, and publish results.

    KPI 4 M8 covers "Accessible and Culturally Safe Delivery" but measurement is "to be negotiated". Commit upfront to measurement by First Nations partners with results published in the Annual Report.

    Where to look→RFT Att. A KPI 4 M8 · §01 above
For Service 5 · CALD & Deaf Communities 10sessions / yr · 15 languages + Auslan
  1. 01Auslan as first-class delivery, not post-translation.

    CALD in the RFT explicitly includes the Deaf community. Auslan-first content design — signing as primary mode, not captioning over spoken English. Deaf facilitators should be Deaf community members, not hearing interpreters.

    Where to look→RFT B.5.1 · Deaf Australia
  2. 02Identify the 15 languages by demand data, not assumption.

    Community selection must be "based on identified needs, reliable data sources, and informed by CALD stakeholder representatives". Use ABS census, school census, DHA migration data and website analytics — and consult NEDA before committing.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 6 · NEDA Alliance
  3. 03Differentiate new-arrival from established CALD families.

    A new-arrival refugee family and an established Cantonese-speaking family with school-age children have radically different relationships to the school system. Design at least two streams: new to the system, and established but in-language.

    Where to look→NEDA submissions · NESB profiles
  4. 04Map cultural understandings of autism per community.

    Autism is understood differently across cultures — pathologised, spiritualised, invisible. Translate concepts, not just words. Pre-workshop community consultation, before content is written.

    Where to look→A&D 2023 Rec 6 · SAC 2022 Rec 8
  5. 05NEDA Alliance as the standing CALD forum partner.

    NEDA is the peak body for CALD people with disability and is named in Service 7's forum list. Route community selection, content review and evaluation through them.

    Where to look→RFT 7B · NEDA Alliance
  6. 06Co-presenter model: language fluency plus lived experience.

    The RFT requires facilitators with CALD experience and community language. Pair them with Autistic or parent co-presenters — bilingual and lived-experience-led, not one or the other.

    Where to look→RFT B.5.2 facilitator requirement
For Service 6 · Online Learning & Information 99.95%uptime · MVP 1 Jul 2026 · WCAG 2.1 AA
  1. 01One learning record across PP and the microcredential.

    PP modules and the microcredential are funded as complements. Single sign-on, shared assessment evidence, unified certificate framework — from day one, not as a retrofit.

    Where to look→RFT B.1.49 · Aust. Gov. Microcredential
  2. 02Statement of Rights as a reusable web component.

    Build the DSE 25 Statement of Rights boxes once as a component. Surface on parent landing, educator landing, every workshop module, every resource related to enrolment/participation/curriculum/support services/anti-bullying.

    Where to look→DSE 25 ss 4.1A–8.1A
  3. 03Translation as an upstream content design parameter.

    Resources for 15+ languages can't be written for English-fluent readers and translated after the fact. Short sentences, concrete examples, plain syntax. Consult NAATI translators at writing stage, not translation stage.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 6 · NAATI
  4. 04Community of Practice moderated by paid Autistic moderators.

    The S 1C CoP is an obvious place for Autistic-led leadership to be visible. A paid moderator team, not volunteers — keeps the space safe, on-topic, NAS co-design-aligned. Honoraria as line items.

    Where to look→NAS Co-design p.21 · RFT 1C
  5. 05Analytics tied to KPI measures, not vanity metrics.

    Service 6 analytics should be designed to measure the 8 KPIs (cohort completion, accessibility, language uptake, regional reach) — not page views. Cohort metadata at registration, with consent.

    Where to look→RFT Att. A · 4 KPIs / 8 measures
  6. 06Brand redesign as a co-design output, not a vendor brief.

    The RFT requires user testing of the new brand with Autistic users, families and education authorities. Co-design the visual identity — not a vendor deck. PP's brand is its most visible artefact; its redesign is the most visible co-design.

    Where to look→RFT 6A · PWDA Co-Design Programming
For Service 7 · Stakeholder Engagement 4+standing forums · annual public report
  1. 01Separate Autistic-led organisations from autism peak bodies.

    Autism peaks (Aspect, Amaze, AAA) and Autistic-led orgs (Reframing Autism, I CAN Network, Yellow Ladybugs) are distinct voices. Folding them into a single forum routinely dilutes Autistic-led voice.

    Where to look→NAS Statement on Language p.8
  2. 02Paid participation; honoraria as line items.

    Co-design without payment is extraction. Build honoraria for Autistic and lived-experience family participation into the budget, and show the amounts in the Annual Report.

    Where to look→NAS Co-design principle
  3. 03AITSL channel for accreditation visibility.

    Gen Req 4 requires PL to meet teacher regulator accreditation. AITSL is the federal coordinator and steward of APST and APSP. A direct AITSL channel in PP's forums — not ad-hoc meetings.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 4 · aitsl.edu.au
  4. 04AutismCRC as a standing forum seat.

    The RFT names AutismCRC research in Gen Req 5. AutismCRC at the forum tier — not just content alignment — is defensible and distinctive.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 5 · autismcrc.com.au
  5. 05Annual public stakeholder report.

    Stakeholder engagement is currently reported privately to the department. A public report turns it into a transparent artefact and gives stakeholders visibility of how their feedback shaped the program.

    Where to look→RFT B.1.131 reporting
  6. 06Cohort representation explicit on every forum.

    The NAS names 12 priority cohorts. Design every forum with explicit cohort representation as a target — and name any unrepresented cohort in the Annual Report. Intersectionality as a structural constraint, not a chapter.

    Where to look→NAS Priority Cohorts pp.12–15
For Service 8 · Data & Continuous Improvement 8measures · 4 KPI indicators
  1. 01Use F-Words as the outcome framework.

    F-Words (Fitness, Functioning, Friends, Family, Fun, Future) give Phase 5 a child-functional outcome vocabulary that goes beyond participation counts. Align M1–M3 measures to F-Words in the Data & CI Plan.

    Where to look→S&S 2024 F-Words p.5
  2. 02Autistic-led evaluation methodology, co-published.

    KPI 4 M8 should be measured by Autistic and cohort-representative evaluators, not solely the Tenderer. Publish methodology alongside results — open-methods evaluation, NAS co-design aligned.

    Where to look→RFT Att. A KPI 4 M8
  3. 03School-leader self-evaluation using SAC goal-tests.

    M3 Whole School Capability rests on pre/post assessment by school leaders. The SAC goal-tests (relevant, safe, desired, feasible) give them a scaffold, and connect S 3 and S 8 measurement.

    Where to look→SAC 2022 Section 2
  4. 04Cohort-disaggregated reporting from day one.

    Collect cohort metadata (with consent and care) at registration. Report disaggregated from day one, not year 2. Creates a baseline for tracking Phase 5's reach to priority cohorts over time.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 6 · NAS Cohorts pp.12–15
  5. 05Participant feedback loop visible to participants.

    A simple "you said / we changed" page on the website, updated after each survey cycle. The smallest co-design loop — converts evaluation from extraction to dialogue.

    Where to look→RFT Gen Req 1 lived experience
  6. 06Open methodology, transparent measurement.

    Where M1–M8 calculations are "to be negotiated", commit upfront to publishing the agreed calculation, minimum performance level, and data source on the website.

    Where to look→RFT Att. A measure definitions
For Service 9 · Targeted Projects (on request) —as required · capacity reserved
  1. 01Reserve capacity for the NAS Autism Health Roadmap.

    The NAS's fourth outcome area — Health & Mental Health — is being developed as a separate roadmap by Health and Aged Care. When it lands, expect new priorities at the school/health interface (school nurses, mental health first aid, late-identification).

    Where to look→NAS p.18 · Health & Mental Health roadmap
  2. 02Reserve capacity for Royal Commission-driven priorities.

    DRC's inclusive-education recommendations will roll out over Phase 5. Reserve capacity and a standing process for translating recommendations into content scope.

    Where to look→DRC Final Report · Vol. 7 Inclusive Education
  3. 03Reserve for microcredential gaps.

    As the microcredential is delivered, gaps will emerge. Build the working relationship in year 1 so S 9 can fill gaps as they're identified.

    Where to look→Aust. Gov. Microcredential (forthcoming)
  4. 04Be ready for sector-emerging priorities.

    Generative AI, neuro-affirming classroom design, post-pandemic school refusal, late-identification — all candidates. The skill needed is rapid scoping, not advance prediction.

    Where to look→RFT B.9.1 Targeted Projects scope
  5. 05Stand-alone research for emerging cohorts.

    NAS names cohorts where education-specific evidence is thin: LGBTQIA+, older Autistic people, very high support needs, AAC users. S 9 is the right vehicle for co-designed peer-reviewed research.

    Where to look→NAS Priority Cohorts pp.10–15
