Positive Partnerships · Phase 5 · Service 1 (Professional Learning)

Initial Phase 5 Content Ideas.

An early draft of the content thinking for a Phase 5 Professional Learning workshop — the themes the room kept circling back to, and a first attempt at sorting what could sit before, during, and after the live session. Built from notes; nothing here is set.

§ 01 · Core framing

Design principles the team kept returning to.

Before content, the room agreed on a set of principles for how Phase 5 PL content should feel and operate.

01

Start with a shared foundational block before role-specific content.

02

Make it theme-based, not jargon-heavy, and grounded in everyday language rather than internal terminology.

03

Keep asking: what does someone walk out able to do tomorrow?

04

Avoid trying to teach everything about autism in the room — some material should sit in pre-work, intro resources, or post resources.

05

Use themes, not a long list of disconnected autism topics. Pair each theme with practical strategies, tools, resources.

06

Make sure teachers leave with something usable — a tool, resource, strategy, something they can apply the next day.

§ 02 · Workshop themes

Themes a Service 1 workshop could be structured around.

The big organising ideas the room proposed for the foundational content of the Professional Learning workshop. Each theme is paired with the specific topics the room said sit inside it.

6 themes
Theme 01

How the autistic brain learns.

One of the strongest organising ideas. A way to bring together how students take in and organise information — and links directly to classroom action, not just theory.

What sits inside
  • Sensory processing
  • Executive functioning
  • Cognitive processing
  • Bottom-up / top-down processing
  • Cognitive processing style — attention tunnel, deep focus, flow (positive and negative impacts)
  • The dynamic / changing support needs depending on capacity (fluctuating capacity)
Theme 02

Inclusive education & inclusive classroom culture.

Start broad with what an inclusive classroom or school is for. Frame inclusion as good for all students, not only autistic students — connected to school purpose, belonging, access, and students being able to contribute what they bring.

What sits inside
  • What is school for? Who is school for?
  • Universal Design for Learning — what does inclusion look like?
  • Disability Standards for Education
  • Teacher attitudes towards education
  • Building an inclusive school environment / school environment design
  • Response-to-intervention framework
Theme 03

Neuro-affirming culture.

What this means in practice, in plain language. Avoid overloading teachers with terms — help them understand difference, needs, and support in practical ways.

What sits inside
  • What does neuro-affirming culture look like in a school?
  • Neurodiversity / neuro-affirming language
  • Language: definitions — neuroinclusion, autism, neurodivergence
  • Medical model vs social model (stepping in / out depending on context)
  • Acknowledgement / recognition — school ‘achievements’ and ableism
  • Checklist audit of a neurodiversity-affirming classroom / school
Theme 04

Barriers and opportunities.

A way to move away from deficit thinking and toward what in the environment or system is getting in the way.

Framed through
  • Interpersonal barriers
  • Environmental barriers
  • Systemic barriers
  • Barriers autistic students can face in the school environment
  • What actions in the classroom have an outsized impact on the teacher experience? (behaviour / student shutdown etc.)
Theme 05

The autistic experience of school.

Not just classroom learning, but the whole day. A resource that helps educators understand school from the student’s perspective across the whole environment, especially for transitions like Year 7.

Across the whole day
  • Drop-off
  • Transitions (including secondary — many more transitions to plan for)
  • Recess and lunch
  • Playgrounds
  • Unstructured time
  • End-of-day exhaustion
  • Whole school environment / classroom / transition points / lunch & outdoor areas
Theme 06

How young people connect.

How young people relate to learning and to others — the social and communication side of the school day. Surfaced as a thread to bring alongside the cognitive and environmental themes.

What sits inside
  • Social and communication differences — what does this mean in real life
  • Double empathy
  • Masking
  • Stimming
  • Student voice
  • Talking to all students about neurodiversity and neurodivergence
§ 03 · Before · During · After

Where each piece of content might sit.

A first cut at sorting content into before the workshop, in the workshop, and after the workshop — the room’s “in the room / pre-loaded / optional follow-up” conversation made visible.

Phase 01 · Before

Pre-workshop.

Pre-learning, intro resources, the “101” that doesn’t need to take up live time.

Foundations to pre-load
  • Raw stats / prevalence / some intro definitions
  • Some 101-style autism background material
  • Pre-learning module / intro resources
Language & definitions
  • Definitions and language (neuroinclusion, autism, neurodivergence)
  • What is included in pre-workshop learning vs in the workshop?
Context-setting stats
  • Statistics that relate to the workshop (e.g. First Nations workshop stats to give a sense of the autism landscape)
  • Prevalence and statistics around autism
→
Phase 02 · During

In the workshop.

Theme-led, low-jargon, paired with strategies — broad, practical, inclusive, tied to how autistic students experience learning and school.

Foundational block (the six themes above)
  • How the autistic brain learns
  • Inclusive education & classroom culture
  • Neuro-affirming culture
  • Barriers and opportunities
  • The autistic experience of school
  • How young people connect
A conceptual bridge
  • A stronger bridge between current Session 1 and Session 2 — briefly framing autism within the broader context of human difference, disability, ableism, and the role environments play in enabling or disabling participation
Practical strategies & take-aways
  • Take-away resources and strategies — visual supports, executive functioning templates etc.
  • Toolbox that teachers can take away
  • Activities in the workshop to create strategies and resources
  • Teachers checking in with the student themselves and family before implementing strategies
Diagnosis (kept light)
  • Diagnosis / late diagnosis / gendered diagnosis patterns
  • Self-identification / professional identification
  • One approach: share the medical model and then the social model — not either/or, but about stepping in/out depending on context and navigating systems. Doesn’t need to be a big piece but can be very useful to include
Sensory (lighter touch, refer on)
  • Sensory is highly valued but could be a briefer topic and then refer to webinar
Safety & regulation (optional module candidate)
  • Dealing with dangerous scenarios — safety for young people, families, educators
  • Fight / flight / freeze / fawn responses
  • Meltdown and responses
→
Phase 03 · After

Community of practice & resources.

A resource library / menu / bento-box of supports for deeper follow-up topics — responsive to new ideas participants bring.

Resource library / menu
  • Resource library / menu / bento-box style set of supports
  • Deeper dives on specific topics via follow-up resources rather than the workshop
  • Reference webinars (e.g. executive functioning, predictive processing)
  • Extension resources — “go to this webinar that’s coming up” etc.
Community of practice
  • Be responsive to new resources / ideas that participants provide us
  • Links between workbook content and the community of practice
  • Real-time feedback on delivery
Optional modules
  • Some safety-scenario / regulation content as an optional module
§ 04 · Tensions

What the room kept getting stuck on.

The repeated tension the team recognised throughout the conversation.

Unresolved
There is a lot of important content, but the group kept recognising that the foundational block cannot hold everything. The team was actively trying to distinguish:
  • What must be in the room
  • What can be pre-loaded
  • What can be optional follow-up
There was concern that the current brainstorm was becoming too broad, and that prioritisation will be needed next.
§ 05 · Converging direction

Where the room seemed to land.

Three layers, then a short synthesis of the foundational-content direction.

Layer 01

Foundational

Broad, practical, inclusive, theme-led, low-jargon, tied to how autistic students experience learning and school.

Layer 02

Role / cohort-specific

A second layer of more role-specific or cohort-specific application.

Layer 03

Optional resources / deeper dives

Additional optional resources and deeper dives, sitting outside the core session.

Best short synthesis of the foundational direction
  1. Start with inclusive education and neuro-affirming culture
  2. Explain how autistic students experience learning and school
  3. Cover the key mechanisms behind that experience — sensory, executive functioning, cognitive processing, environment
  4. Frame it through barriers, access, and practical support
  5. Keep the language simple
  6. Make sure it leads to concrete classroom action
